1 Sign up with your office.

Anyone at Studio Gang can play. On the Sign-up tab, enter your name and pick the office you're representing.

2 Two leagues from four offices.

Once sign-ups close, the four offices are paired into two leagues. Each league is two offices joined as one — so the four offices line up as two-against-two for the entire tournament.

3 Each league needs at least 8 sign-ups.

The pairing is random, with one guardrail: each league's combined sign-ups should reach at least eight, so it can field a full bracket. When sign-ups close, the randomizer considers all three possible pairings of the four offices and picks at random among those where both sides have eight or more.

Example: Chicago 7, New York 4, San Francisco 3, Paris 4. The valid pairing is Chicago + San Francisco (10) vs New York + Paris (8). The other splits leave one league short of eight, so the randomizer won't pick them.

If total sign-ups are below sixteen and no pairing can satisfy the rule, the randomizer falls back to the most balanced split.

4 Eight players per league.

Each league fields exactly eight players at the tournament — sixteen total. The winning league earns the year's bragging rights for its two offices.

5 Office quotas scale with sign-ups.

Each office's share of its league's eight seats is proportional to how many of its people signed up.

Example: Chicago signs up 10 and San Francisco signs up 5. Their joint league of 8 fills with 5 from Chicago and 3 from San Francisco.

6 Gamemaster locks the roster before camp.

When an office has more sign-ups than its quota, the office's gamemaster picks who plays — through an office-wide sub-match, a lottery, or any other method they choose — and logs the final roster by July 28, 2026, the day before Camp Wandawega opens.

1 Random doubles teams within each league.

At the tournament, each league's eight players are randomly paired into four doubles teams — eight teams total entering the bracket. You don't pick your partner; the bracket draws them.

2 Single-elimination bracket.

Win and move on, lose and you're out. Four matches in the quarterfinals, two in the semis, one final — straight to the trophy.

3 Round 1 is always cross-league.

The bracket is drawn so that every quarterfinal is one team from one league against a team from the other. Teams from the same league don't face each other in Round 1 — intra-league matchups only become possible from the semifinals onward.

4 Best of 3, games to 11.

Every match is best-of-3 games. Each game goes to the first team to reach 11 points, with a 2-point margin — at 10–10 you keep playing until one team leads by two.

5 Serve switches every 2 points.

Olympic-style. The right to serve passes to the other team after every two points are played, regardless of who scored them. At 10–10 and beyond, the serve switches after every single point.

6 Champions take the trophy — and the league wins the year.

The team that wins the final lifts the trophy and brings their league the win for the year. Two offices share the bragging rights; one team gets the silverware.

Frequently Asked

When and where is the tournament?

The Doubles Ping-Pong Tournament is held at the Studio Gang summer retreat at Camp Wandawega. Camp opens July 29, 2026; the tournament runs on-site during the retreat.

Key dates: Sign-ups close June 16, 2026. League pairing happens right after that. Final office rosters lock by July 28, 2026, the day before camp opens.

How are doubles teams paired?

Once sign-ups close, the four offices are split into two leagues — each league joins two offices as one. Players are then paired into random doubles teams within their league when the bracket is generated.

How are the four offices paired into two leagues?

Once sign-ups close, the randomizer enumerates all three possible ways to split the four offices into two pairs, then picks at random among the pairings where each league has at least eight sign-ups (enough to field a full bracket).

Example: Chicago 7, New York 4, San Francisco 3, Paris 4. Pairing Chicago with San Francisco (10) against New York with Paris (8) is the only split where both leagues clear eight, so it's the one the randomizer uses. The other two splits would leave one league short, so they're skipped.

If total sign-ups are below sixteen and no split can satisfy the rule, the randomizer falls back to the most balanced pairing (smallest gap between the two side-totals).

What if my office has more sign-ups than spots?

Each office's share of its league's eight seats is proportional to how many people signed up. Example: Chicago signs up 10 and San Francisco signs up 5 — their joint league of eight fills with 5 from Chicago and 3 from SF.

If your office has more sign-ups than its quota, the office's gamemaster decides who plays — through an office-wide sub-match, a lottery, or any other method — and logs the final roster by July 28, 2026 (the day before Camp Wandawega opens on July 29).

Can I pick my own doubles partner?

No. Teams are formed by random pairing within each league when the bracket is generated. This keeps things fair and shakes up the matchups across offices.

What's the match format on tournament day?

Single-elimination doubles. Every match is a best-of-3, with each game played to 11 points (win by 2). Service swaps every 2 points, Olympic-style — and after 10–10 it swaps every point.

The bracket is also drawn so that every Round 1 match is one team from one league against a team from the other; intra-league matchups only become possible from the semifinals onward.

Can I sign up after sign-ups close?

Sign-ups close on June 16, 2026. After that, the Sign-up tab is locked while league pairing and roster selection happen. If you missed the window, talk to your office's gamemaster — sometimes there's still room.

What if I can't make it after signing up?

Let your office's gamemaster know as early as possible so they can adjust the roster. Final rosters lock on July 28, 2026, the day before camp opens.

Are there equipment or dress-code requirements?

Nope. Paddles and balls are provided at camp. Wear whatever you'd play in — though league-themed colors are always encouraged.
